## 1. Optimal market-making theory for binary/bounded contracts: what spread and inventory formulas follow?

### Takeaway
The standard toolkit is Avellaneda-Stoikov (AS) for inventory, Glosten-Milgrom for adverse selection, and Kyle and Shin for informed flow. It has been adapted to binary contracts by working in log-odds (logit) space and adding a penalty at settlement. Two recent papers (Dalen 2025, Feil and Nendel July 2026) give the formulas. Empirical fits of logit-AS to real Polymarket order books are weak and unstable. Use AS for the structure of quotes (skew against inventory, widen with time remaining and volatility) and calibrate the numbers from your own markouts.

### Cited Findings
- **Classic AS (2008), textbook formulas, stated from the original paper and not from a fetched source.** Reservation price r = s − q·γ·σ²·(T−t). Optimal total spread δ_a+δ_b = γσ²(T−t) + (2/γ)·ln(1+γ/k). Here q is inventory, γ is risk aversion, σ is mid volatility, and k is the decay rate of fill intensity λ(δ)=A·e^(−kδ) as quote distance δ grows — [Avellaneda & Stoikov 2008, Quantitative Finance](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697680701381228)
- **Dalen, "Toward Black-Scholes for Prediction Markets: A Unified Kernel and Market Maker's Handbook" (arXiv 2510.15205, Oct 2025).** Works in logit units. The mid is a log-odds state, order arrival intensity decays exponentially with distance from the mid, the reservation quote shifts against inventory, and the spread widens with time remaining and risk aversion. Quotes are mapped back to probabilities with the logistic function p = 1/(1+e^(−x)). The paper proposes a logit jump-diffusion as the pricing kernel for event contracts — [arXiv 2510.15205](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15205v2)
- **Feil & Nendel, "Optimal Market Making in Prediction Markets" (arXiv 2607.17991, July 2026).** A stochastic-control setup. The price is a conditional probability generated by a transformed latent belief diffusion, and the latent belief equals the log-odds of the market probability. The market maker maximises expected terminal wealth while managing both mark-to-market inventory risk and settlement (jump-to-0/1) risk. The paper links the setup to Hanson's LMSR — [arXiv 2607.17991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.17991); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2607.17991.html)
- **Comillas master's thesis (Sánchez García) adapting AS to Polymarket binaries.** Quotes are bounded in [0,1], historical volatility is replaced by Bernoulli variance p(1−p), inventory is normalised, and there is a terminal penalty for settlement at 0 or 1. The pure market-making backtest earned about 155 USDC per market on the test set. Results were sensitive to tail events and the sample was small; the author presents the model as a proposal, not proof — [Comillas repository](https://repositorio.comillas.edu/jspui/handle/11531/109132)
- **University of Tartu thesis, the empirical test.** Tested logit-AS on tick data for one Polymarket Bitcoin-strike market (March 2026). Bid-side fill intensity mostly declined with quote distance, as the model predicts, but the fit was weak and unstable across days and price ranges. Ask-side estimates often had the wrong sign. On some days the model-implied spread was **1.8 to 5.4 times the observed spread** — [UT DSpace](https://dspace.ut.ee/items/b6ff7a3f-17e4-4009-86f6-899560034b3a)
- **TUM master's thesis (Parshant Singh, Sept 2025).** Liquidity provision on Polymarket's CLOB (order book) is "significantly more profitable" than on its earlier AMM, where realised impermanent loss eroded most profits. Order-book makers adjust quotes to manage inventory and to collect platform rewards — [TUM thesis PDF](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de/fileadmin/w00cfj/sebis/_my_direct_uploads/20250903_Parshant_MA_Thesis.pdf)
- **Shin model (adverse selection by insiders).** A bookmaker shades longshot odds to limit losses to better-informed insiders. Whelan (2023) generalises it and finds that disagreement among uninformed bettors creates favourite-longshot bias, while insiders lower odds without necessarily worsening the bias. The market collapses if the share of insiders rises above low levels — [UCD WP23/16](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP23_16.pdf); [CEPR DP18315](https://cepr.org/publications/dp18315)

### Inferences
- **Practical binary-AS quoting rule.** Work in logit x = ln(p/(1−p)) around the de-vigged Pinnacle fair value x*.
  - Reservation price: x_r = x* − q·γ·σ_x²·τ, where τ is time to the next information event (for example lineup release or kickoff), not time to expiry.
  - Half-spread: roughly (adverse-selection cost) + (1/γ)·ln(1+γ/k) + fees.
  - In price space a fixed logit spread becomes Δp ≈ p(1−p)·Δx. That means quotes at 10c or 90c should be about 0.36 times as wide in cents as quotes at 50c, for the same logit risk.
- **Glosten-Milgrom gives the floor.** Half-spread ≥ (probability the counterparty is informed) × (expected price jump when informed). Example: if 10% of fills come just before a 4c move, the half-spread must be at least 0.4c plus fees just to break even. Measuring that probability and jump size empirically (from markouts) matters more than solving for γ.
- **Settlement risk dominates at the 1/0 jump.** A $1 binary held to resolution has variance p(1−p) per contract, which is $0.25 at 50c. For a $100k book, unhedged inventory at kickoff (if you do not flatten it) should be sized like a bet, with fractional Kelly applied to your edge versus the Pinnacle close, not like a market-making position.

### Gaps
- No paper found validates logit-AS on sports contracts specifically. All empirical tests found are on crypto or political Polymarket markets.
- No published estimates of the fill-intensity decay k or the informed-flow share for Kalshi or Novig sports books.

## 2. Empirical adverse selection on betting exchanges and prediction markets (Betfair, Kalshi, Polymarket)

### Takeaway
The evidence consistently shows that **makers beat takers**, but makers still lose money at low prices (favourite-longshot bias), and profits are extremely concentrated.
- **Kalshi:** takers lose about 32% on average and makers about 10%, with contracts under 10c losing more than 60%.
- **Polymarket:** the top 1% of profitable users take 76.5% of profits, and winners are mostly limit-order providers.
- **Adverse selection:** informed flow and one-sided order flow predict maker losses in "single-name" markets.
- **Betfair:** price discovery is fast and widely distributed across traders. Fast traders (courtsiders) profit from stale quotes.

### Cited Findings
- **Bürgi, Deng & Whelan, "Makers and Takers: The Economics of the Kalshi Prediction Market"** (UCD WP2025/19, July 2025, revised Sept 2025; CEPR DP20631).
  - Sample of more than 300,000 contracts. The average contract return is about **−20%** before fees.
  - Contracts priced **under 10c lose more than 60%**. Contracts **over 50c earn a small, statistically significant positive return**.
  - Makers earn higher returns than takers, but both show the favourite-longshot pattern.
  - Prices become more accurate as a market nears close, and there is some evidence the bias is shrinking over time.
  - The model explains this with makers being slightly overconfident and a small tendency to overstate small probabilities — [UCD WP2025_19](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf); [CEPR DP20631](https://cepr.org/publications/dp20631)
  - The VoxEU summary gives approximate averages of **takers about −32%, makers about −10%** — [VoxEU column](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market). These are contract-weighted averages and include long-dated non-sports markets.
- **Akey, Grégoire, Harvie & Martineau, "Who Wins and Who Loses in Prediction Markets? Evidence from Polymarket"** (CEPR DP21615).
  - 588 million trades, about $67bn in volume. The **top 1% of users with positive PnL capture 76.5% of profits**.
  - "Successful traders provide liquidity using limit orders that resolve favorably relative to realized outcomes while unsuccessful traders take liquidity using market orders."
  - Monthly performance is only modestly persistent, and insider trading is unlikely to explain the biggest winners — [CEPR DP21615](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21615); [HEC event page](https://www.hec.edu/en/faculty-research/events/who-wins-and-who-loses-prediction-markets-evidence-polymarket)
  - A secondary press write-up (April 2026) quotes 84.1% for the top 1%, using 1.4m users and 70m trades from Nov 2022 to Oct 2025. The version of the paper differs from DP21615 — [CoinNews/coin360 summary](https://coin360.com/news/polymarket-traders-losses-profit-concentration-2026-predictions)
- **Bartlett, "Adverse Selection in Prediction Markets: Evidence from Kalshi"** (Stanford, SSRN, April 2026). Uses 41.6m Kalshi trades.
  - Single-name markets show more informed price impact than broad-based markets.
  - Even so, makers earn **about 2 times more per contract** in single-name markets, because traders overbet YES on markets that mostly settle NO.
  - **One-sided order flow predicts maker losses** in single-name markets but not in broad ones — [Stanford Law](https://law.stanford.edu/?p=564918). The search snippet did not show whether sports are broken out separately.
- **Susquehanna on Kalshi.**
  - Susquehanna Government Products became Kalshi's first dedicated institutional market maker in April 2024 — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240403664852/en/Kalshi-Onboards-Its-First-Dedicated-Institutional-Market-Maker)
  - InGame (June 2026) reported Susquehanna's largest-ever sports loss on the Knicks' NBA Finals Game 4 comeback, mostly from in-game fills at about 5% odds. Market makers lost **about $22.4m before fees on that one game** — [InGame](https://ingame.com/susquehanna-biggest-sports-kalshi-volume-knicks). This is an in-play tail event, a case of jump/settlement risk.
- **Betfair: the 2011 natural experiment (Brown).** Betfair introduced a Premium Charge of up to 60% of profits on the top 0.1% of profitable traders. Brown found "scant decline" in forecasting accuracy relative to a control market, which implies price discovery is spread across many traders — [UEA ePrints](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/51719/)
- **Betfair: fast traders (courtsiding), JEBO 2018.** Courtsiders, who watch live tennis ahead of the TV delay, speed up price discovery. A simple strategy "yields a positive trading return to fast traders after the occurrence of important informational events" — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v156y2018icp126-143.html). This is in-play; the pregame equivalent is a sharp-book move that you have not yet reflected in your quotes.
- **Croxson & Reade, "Information and Efficiency: Goal Arrival in Soccer Betting,"** Economic Journal 124(575), 2014, pp. 62-91 — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/bir/birmec/11-19.html). The full text was not retrieved. The common summary is that Betfair prices update within seconds of goals; this was not verified here.
- **Exchanges vs bookmakers on favourite-longshot bias.**
  - Smith, Paton & Vaughan Williams (Economica 2006), using matched UK horse-racing data, found significantly lower biases on exchanges and favoured an information-based explanation — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/econom/v73y2006i292p673-689.html)
  - Marginson (2010) argues the Shin measure does not apply cleanly to exchanges. Using a "significant mover" test, he finds "known loser" trading may be commonplace on exchanges — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/soecon/v76y2010i4p932-952.html)
- **Betfair Premium Charge and share of winners.**
  - The charge applies only when an account is in profit, its lifetime (later a rolling 60-week) charges are below 20% of gross profits, and it has bet in more than 250 markets.
  - Forum-sourced claims (2005-2009, unverified) put those affected at about 0.5% at launch and 0.1% for the higher rates — [bookmaker-ratings wiki](https://bookmaker-ratings.ru/wiki/premium-charges-betfair/); [SBR forum](https://www.sportsbookreview.com/forum/horse-racing-betting/698661-betfair-increase-premium-charge-upto-60)
- **Stale-quote mechanism.** A Kalshi resting offer becomes stale when news arrives, and makers cannot ban the traders who pick them off (sportsbooks can limit sharp bettors). Competition caps how wide makers can quote — [How Gambling Works substack](https://howgamblingworks.substack.com/p/kalshis-favorite-lie)

### Inferences
- **Where to make.** Being the maker is necessary but not sufficient. Concentrate maker inventory on the side and price range where flow is biased: sell YES on longshots and buy favourites at 50c or above, consistent with Bürgi et al. and Bartlett. Avoid accumulating cheap longshot YES.
- **Cancel / kill trigger.** One-sided flow is the measurable warning sign. Trigger on a run of same-side fills or a net-flow imbalance in a short window, since Bartlett finds one-sided flow predicts maker losses.
- **Who you are up against.** Profits are concentrated in a small elite, so a solo trader competes directly with SIG-type firms on major markets. Smaller, less-covered markets are where a $100k operator may still find room, which is consistent with Bartlett's higher per-contract maker earnings in single-name markets.

### Gaps
- No published post-fill markout curves (for example 1s/10s/60s/5min) for Kalshi, Novig or Polymarket sports contracts were found. Betfair markout studies by time-to-start were also not found.
- No current official Betfair statistic on the percentage of winning accounts.
- Brown & Yang (Review of Finance) was not retrieved.

## 3. How accurate is Pinnacle's close as fair value; margin removal; speed of sharp lines and odds-feed latency

### Takeaway
Pinnacle (and multi-book consensus) prices are good fair-value estimates. Betting systematically against soft-book deviations from that consensus has been profitable in long backtests: about 3.5-3.8% realised ROI on European football, and Kaunitz et al.'s 2017 strategy until the accounts were limited.
- **CLV:** beating the close predicts profit only noisily (Whelan).
- **Margin removal:** proportional (multiplicative) de-vigging is the market default and looks adequate for odds between 35% and 65%. Use Shin or power methods at the extremes, where favourite-longshot bias matters.
- **Feed latency:** no independent millisecond benchmarks exist. Vendor claims put delivery in the seconds range, so stale-quote risk against faster sharps is structural.

### Cited Findings
- **Pinnacle as ground truth on European football (blog; uses Buchdahl's football-data.co.uk data, about 20 books, 22 leagues).**
  - Betting best-market odds when they beat Pinnacle pre-closing fair value: expected ROI **3.8%, realised 3.6% over 14 seasons**.
  - Using closing odds (2019/20 onward only): expected 4.3%, realised 3.5%.
  - Performance worsened from 2023/24 — [Networked substack, "A view from the Pinnacle"](https://networked.substack.com/p/a-view-from-the-pinnacle)
  - The close agreement between expected and realised ROI suggests Pinnacle fair values are close to calibrated.
- **Kaunitz, Zhong & Kreiner (2017), "Beating the bookies with their own numbers."**
  - Used the average of bookmaker odds as a probability estimate and bet on outliers.
  - The paper reportedly gives a chance below 1 in a billion that random betting would return at least $98,865 from 56,435 bets. MIT Technology Review also cites an 8.5% return without clear context.
  - Bookmakers "started to severely limit our accounts" within months — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2017/10/19/67760/the-secret-betting-strategy-that-beats-online-bookmakers/)
- **Datagolf on golf matchups (2019 onward, 106k matchups).** Proportional margin removal is "sufficient". Blind returns are near zero at each book, and the favourite-longshot bias plays little role for odds between 35% and 65% — [Datagolf](https://datagolf.com/how-sharp-are-bookmakers)
- **Whelan, "The Truth about Closing Line Value."**
  - Positive-CLV bets did better than negative-CLV bets, but positive CLV does not guarantee profit.
  - Per the search summary, only the top decile of moneyline bets turned CLV into meaningful profit, and one high-CLV group made just a +0.2% profit — [karlwhelan.com](https://www.karlwhelan.com/?p=2595). The full tables were not retrieved.
- **Odds-feed latency claims (all vendor or competitor claims, unverified).**
  - OpticOdds is marketed as "under 800ms" delivery over SSE — [SportsGameOdds comparison](https://sportsgameodds.com/compare/opticodds)
  - SharpAPI puts OddsJam's freshness at about 5-10s (WebSocket). It says Pinnacle's CDN caches for about 7 minutes on live events, but aggressive polling gets effective freshness to 3-5s. It puts The Odds API at a 30-60s collection interval — [SharpAPI technical guide](https://sharpapi.io/learn/how-live-is-real-time-odds-data)
  - OddsPapi states The Odds API and SportsGameOdds have a 60s delay — [OddsPapi latency page](https://oddspapi.io/coverage/latency)
  - Total latency = book poll interval + processing + delivery. Push transport matters less than how often the vendor collects upstream — [SharpAPI](https://sharpapi.io/learn/how-live-is-real-time-odds-data)

### Inferences
- **Fair-value construction.** Use de-vigged Pinnacle odds:
  - multiplicative for prices between 0.35 and 0.65;
  - Shin or power methods for longshots, because proportional de-vigging overstates longshot probability. That direction is consistent with exchange longshots being overpriced.
  - Pinnacle's two-way margin is about 2% on major markets (forum estimate) — [SBR forum](https://www.sportsbookreview.com/forum/handicapper-think-tank/506237-beating-pinny-s-line-guarantee-profit)
  - That leaves roughly ±1% uncertainty in "fair" just from de-vig method choice at 50c. Your quoted half-spread should not be tighter than this, plus fees, unless markouts prove otherwise.
- **Feed speed and cancel rules.** With feed staleness in seconds (3-10s typical claims), assume any Pinnacle move of at least 1-2c of implied probability will be traded against your quotes before you see it. Practical rules:
  - pull quotes on any upstream move above a threshold;
  - pull quotes on a "fill before feed update" pattern;
  - widen around known news windows (NFL inactives about 90 minutes before kickoff, NBA injury reports and lineups about 30 minutes before tip).
- **Pinnacle can lag too.** Pinnacle limits are low early in the week and in small markets. Its early lines are less efficient than its close, so anchoring to an early Pinnacle line carries more model risk than anchoring to a near-close line.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed calibration study of Pinnacle's closing lines in US sports was found in this pass.
- No independent latency benchmark for TheRundown, OpticOdds, OddsJam or a direct Pinnacle API.
- No study of the lead-lag between Kalshi or Novig prices and Pinnacle.

## 4. Realised returns of market makers in sports and prediction markets

### Takeaway
There are no direct public P&L disclosures from Kalshi or Novig market makers. The best evidence is indirect:
- **Kalshi:** makers lose about 10% on average across all contracts versus takers losing about 32% (contract-return basis, pre-fee). Makers in favourite-priced (≥50c) contracts earn small positive returns.
- **Polymarket:** limit-order providers dominate profits.
- **Tail risk:** a single in-play event cost makers about $22.4m on Kalshi.

The 1% spread floor Blockworks observed on March Madness, minus Kalshi maker fees and adverse selection, suggests net margins of a fraction of a cent per contract for pregame sports.

### Cited Findings
- Kalshi maker vs taker returns, about −10% vs −32% — [VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/economics-kalshi-prediction-market); [UCD WP](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf)
- Kalshi single-name markets: makers earn about 2 times more per contract than in broad markets — [Bartlett, Stanford](https://law.stanford.edu/?p=564918)
- Kalshi makers lost about $22.4m pre-fee on Knicks NBA Finals Game 4 (June 2026), Susquehanna's largest sports loss — [InGame](https://ingame.com/susquehanna-biggest-sports-kalshi-volume-knicks)
- When Susquehanna arrived (2024), Kalshi's CEO said traders could move 100,000 shares at once versus 1,000 before — [Straight to the Point substack](https://straighttothepoint.substack.com/p/a-sportsbook-in-sheeps-clothing)
- Polymarket rewards-based market making:
  - Orders score only if they meet rewardsMinSize and sit within rewardsMaxSpread of the mid, with diminishing returns once you dominate the pool — [poly-maker docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/warproxxx/poly-maker); [Tread docs](https://docs.tread.fi/prediction-markets/polymarket/market-making-on-polymarket)
  - About $70k in sponsored rewards on a single market drew makers in and raised the cost of moving the price by about 10 times — [Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/sponsored-liquidity-rewards-jesus-market)
- Comillas thesis backtest: about 155 USDC per Polymarket market from AS market making. Small sample, sensitive to tails — [Comillas](https://repositorio.comillas.edu/jspui/handle/11531/109132)
- **Kalshi fees.**
  - Taker fee = ceil(0.07·C·P·(1−P)); maker fee = ceil(0.0175·C·P·(1−P)).
  - At 50c that is 1.75c per contract for a taker and about 0.44c for a maker. Cancels are free.
  - The maker fee formula changed around July 1 (previously a flat 0.25c per contract on markets with maker fees). Sources disagree on a July 2026 multiplier — [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-fees); [InGame](https://www.ingame.com/kalshis-change-may-increase-fee-sports-traders/)
- **Novig.**
  - Maker fills and pregame trades are reportedly zero-fee; live and parlay trades carry taker fees.
  - Annualised volume is above $4bn after 10x growth in 2025; it raised a $75m Series B in Feb 2026.
  - Its operating entity, Ludlow Exchange, reportedly got CFTC DCM status on 16 June 2026 and relaunched nationally on 4 Aug 2026 (47 states) — [Gambling.com review](https://www.gambling.com/us/prediction-markets/reviews/novig); [FXStreet press release](https://www.fxstreet.com/press-releases/novig-raises-75m-series-b-to-build-a-trader-first-sports-prediction-market-202602181435)
  - These are affiliate and press sources; check the official fee schedule.

### Inferences
- **Rough Kalshi economics (illustrative, not sourced).** Quote 1c wide around fair at 50c, so the gross edge per round-trip pair is 1c. Subtract 2 × 0.44c maker fees, leaving about 0.12c net before adverse selection. Pregame market making on the tightest Kalshi NFL/NBA books is therefore close to zero-margin for a fee-paying maker. Edge has to come from:
  - wider markets (≥3c spreads);
  - fee-free venues (Novig pregame makers);
  - maker rebates or incentive programs;
  - directional skew toward Pinnacle fair value, which is CLV capture rather than spread capture.
- **Return expectations for a $100k book.** If the net is 0.2-0.5% of notional matched and you turn over $2-5m per month (a guess based on capital turnover across many games), monthly P&L would be about $4k-25k. Single tail events (correlated news, in-play) can wipe out months of this. Treat these figures as hypotheses to test with small size, not as evidence.

### Gaps
- No Kalshi market-maker program terms or rebate amounts, and no SIG volume share, were found.
- No Novig market-maker program details were found.
- No public net margin per $ traded for any sports exchange maker.
- No Betfair trader P&L datasets were found.

## 5. Quantified heuristics: spreads, widening near events, markouts by time-to-start, correlated news risk, Kelly fractions

### Takeaway
Sports spreads on Kalshi (March Madness, per Blockworks) tighten to about **1c roughly 48 hours before tipoff** and stay near that floor. Displayed depth falls about 76% in-game as makers protect against adverse selection. Practitioner advice is to make when the spread is 3c or wider and take when it is 1-2c close to kickoff. Injury and player-availability markets were pulled by Polymarket and Kalshi under CFTC pressure in Aug-Sept 2026, which highlights how sensitive these contracts are to news. No sports markout curves or Kelly-for-market-making studies were found.

### Cited Findings
- **March Madness on Kalshi (Blockworks).** Spreads start wide, tighten to about 1% about 48h before tipoff and stay near that floor. Displayed depth rises sharply pregame and drops about 76% in-game — [Blockworks Research](https://app.blockworksresearch.com/unlocked/from-betting-to-trading-how-kalshi-is-reshaping-sports-markets). This is from the search summary; the full page was not fetched.
- **NFL on Kalshi.** NFL game markets are the most liquid on the exchange. Taker fees peak at 50c, where NFL sides usually trade. Practitioner advice: make when the spread is ≥3c, take when it is 1-2c near kickoff — [OddsShopper, NFL on Kalshi](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/how-to-trade-nfl-on-kalshi); [OddsShopper liquidity](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-liquidity-best-sports-markets). This is opinion.
- **Injury markets.**
  - Polymarket withdrew an NFL player-availability (Mahomes) contract at the CFTC's request in late Aug 2026; Kalshi initially kept similar markets — [CasinoBeats](https://casinobeats.com/2026/08/31/polymarket-pulls-nfl-player-availability-bets-at-cftc-request-kalshi-keeps-them/)
  - Kalshi later removed athlete injury and return-timeline markets at the CFTC's request (early Sept 2026) — [CDC Gaming](https://cdcgaming.com/brief/kalshi-removes-athlete-injury-markets-after-cftc-intervention/)
  - The CFTC's proposed rules call injury-linked markets "contrary to the public interest" (same sources).
- **Earlier-information trading on exchanges.** In-play exchange prices start updating before the public receives news, implying insiders. That finding is from tennis — [RePEc listing, Brown](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/eurjfi/v22y2016i2p105-119.html)
- **Pregame insider trading by race class.** In UK horse racing, insiders appear more in lower-class races and bet earlier — [Shing 2005 via search summary](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/fin/papers/0412/0412010.PDF). This suggests less-liquid or lower-profile events carry more informed flow early on.

### Inferences
These are proposed rules for a $100k solo market maker, derived from the evidence above. They need calibration with your own fills.

- **Spread.**
  - Quote at least max(2c, 2 × (maker fee + de-vig uncertainty)) on liquid NFL/NBA sides at 50c. Do not try to match SIG's 1c floor unless your markouts are positive at 1c.
  - Quote 3-5c in small markets: props, college, lower leagues.
  - Use logit scaling (about p(1−p)/0.25) away from 50c.
  - Skew asymmetrically: sell longshot YES more aggressively (favourite-longshot evidence).
- **Size.**
  - Per-quote clip of about 0.5-1% of bankroll ($500-1,000 notional).
  - Per-game net inventory cap of about 2-3% of bankroll ($2-3k).
  - Aggregate correlated exposure cap (same team or slate, same news source) of about 10%.
  - Kelly: treat inventory held into settlement as a bet. With edge ε versus Pinnacle fair at price p, full Kelly is f* ≈ ε/(1−p) for YES. Use 0.1-0.25 Kelly, given model uncertainty in fair value of about ±1c. No published market-making-specific Kelly guidance was found.
- **Cancel rules.**
  1. Cancel all quotes on a game when the Pinnacle de-vigged fair value moves at least 1c, or when the feed goes stale for more than 5-10s.
  2. Cancel when you get N same-side fills (for example 2-3) within 60s, or a fill that comes before an upstream move. This is the one-sided-flow warning from Bartlett.
  3. Widen or pull 5-15 minutes before known news windows: NFL inactives at about T−90min, NBA injury and lineup at about T−30min, MLB lineups and pitchers, soccer team sheets at about T−60min.
  4. Flatten or stop quoting before kickoff unless you deliberately want to hold the position as a bet. In-play is where the largest maker losses occur (Knicks $22.4m; Betfair courtsiding).
- **Kill criteria.**
  - Stop if the rolling 1-minute and 5-minute markouts (fill price versus later Pinnacle fair value) are negative over at least 200 fills.
  - Stop on a daily loss above 2% of bankroll, or a weekly loss above 5%.
  - Stop if CLV versus the Pinnacle close on held inventory is ≤0 over at least 300 positions. Whelan shows CLV is noisy, so require statistical significance before scaling up rather than treating CLV as proof of edge.
