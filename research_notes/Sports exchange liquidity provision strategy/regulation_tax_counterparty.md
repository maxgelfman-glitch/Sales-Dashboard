## 1. New York enforcement and litigation (Kalshi, Polymarket, Novig, ProphetX, Robinhood)

### Takeaway
New York is one of the most aggressive states. The NYS Gaming Commission sent Kalshi a cease-and-desist (Oct 2025). Kalshi lost its federal injunction bid in S.D.N.Y. (July 7, 2026) and in the Second Circuit (July 27, 2026), and the AG and Governor then sued Kalshi in state court (July 31, 2026; reported ask about $36B) and Polymarket US (Sept 24, 2026). As of early October 2026, secondary guides say Kalshi sports contracts are still live for NY users, but that access could end at any time.

## 2. Federal court rulings and CFTC stance (2026)

### Takeaway
There is a deep circuit split. The **3rd Cir. sided with Kalshi** (NJ, Apr 6, 2026, 2-1). The **9th Cir.** (NV, Aug 28, 2026) and the **6th Cir.** (OH/TN, Sept 25, 2026, unanimous) sided with states. The **4th Cir.** (MD) was argued May 7, 2026 and is undecided. NJ's cert petition (filed Sept 2) is pending, with Kalshi's response due Nov 9, 2026, so a SCOTUS merits decision would likely come by June 2027 at the earliest. The CFTC under Chairman Selig backs exclusive federal jurisdiction. It withdrew the 2024 anti-sports-contract proposal, issued an ANPRM (Mar 12, 2026) and an NPRM (June 10, 2026) revising Rule 40.11, and has intervened against states.

### Cited Findings
- **3rd Cir., KalshiEX v. Flaherty, No. 25-1922 (Apr 6, 2026):** affirmed the PI for Kalshi 2-1 (Chagares, Porter; Roth dissenting). The CFTC has exclusive jurisdiction over swaps traded on a DCM, and field and conflict preemption bar NJ enforcement. The court declined to decide whether the CEA preempts all state gambling law. The dissent argued that Rule 40.11(a)(1) already bars gaming contracts. — [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca3/25-1922/25-1922-2026-04-06.html); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/federal-appeals-court-cftc-jurisdiction-over-sports-event-contracts); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/04/06/appeals-court-blocks-new-jersey-from-shutting-down-kalshi-s-sports-markets)
- **NJ cert petition filed Sept 2, 2026.** Kalshi's response deadline was extended to **Nov 9, 2026**. On **Oct 2, 2026**, gaming regulators filed an amicus brief urging review. A decision could come by June 2027. — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/275556/new-jersey-asks-supreme-court-to-rule-on-kalshi-sports-predictions/); [Yogonet, Oct 8 2026](https://www.yogonet.com/international/news/2026/10/08/126758-gaming-regulators-urge-us-supreme-court-to-hear-kalshi-case-decision-could-come-by-june-2027); [SBC Americas, Oct 7](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/10/07/kalshi-supreme-court-regulator-brief/)
- **9th Cir. (Nevada), Aug 28, 2026:** affirmed dissolution of Kalshi's PI. The CEA preempts only for genuine swaps, and sports contracts are "sports gambling" in substance (Judge Nelson). Kalshi sought rehearing en banc on Sept 9, 2026, which is pending. Robinhood and Crypto.com sought cert from their own 9th Cir. loss. — [Nevada Current](https://nevadacurrent.com/2026/08/28/ninth-circuit-win-for-nevada-over-kalshi-sets-up-potential-supreme-court-showdown/); [Covers](https://www.covers.com/industry/ninth-circuit-rehearing-prediction-market-supreme-court-timeline-september-16-2026); [Gambling.com](https://www.gambling.com/us/news/ninth-circuit-rules-against-kalshi-backs-nevada-gambling-law)

Alternatively, even if they were swaps, the CEA's exclusive-jurisdiction clause does not preempt state gambling law. — [SBC Americas](https://sbcamericas.com/2026/09/25/kalshi-sixth-circuit-sides-oh-tn/); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/policy/2026/09/25/another-appeals-court-rules-against-prediction-market-provider-kalshi-says-sports-contracts-are-subject-to-state-regulations); [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/sixth-circuit-says-states-can-regulate-kalshi-other-prediction-markets/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-09-26-kalshi-loses-appeal-over-ohio-and-tennessee-sports-betting-laws-widening-circuit-split-416937)

(Maryland):** the district court ruled for the state, and the appeal was argued May 7, 2026 and is still pending in early October. — [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/perspectives/kalshi-acts-swiftly-across-pending-litigation-after-adverse-maryland-ruling.html?id=102kz4e); [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/where-is-kalshi-legal)

### Inferences
- New York sits in the 2nd Circuit, which has no merits ruling yet. The S.D.N.Y. denial plus the 6th and 9th Cir. reasoning make a pro-state outcome in NY more likely than not near-term. The 3rd Cir. ruling protects only NJ-facing activity.
- The binding resolution is SCOTUS (OT2026, decision likely by June 2027 if cert is granted). Until then, expect state-by-state geofencing to grow.
- A final CFTC rule would strengthen the conflict-preemption argument but would not bind courts on whether sports contracts are "swaps."

## 4. Tax treatment (US federal and New York, 2026)

### Takeaway
There is no IRS guidance specific to event contracts as of mid-2026. Plausible characterizations are gambling (wagering), capital gain/loss, ordinary income, or (aggressively) Section 1256 60/40. Under the OBBBA, **wagering losses are deductible only up to 90%** of losses (and only up to winnings, and only if itemizing) for tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2025. That rule bites only if event contracts are treated as wagering. 1099 reporting is inconsistent, so traders must keep their own records.

### Cited Findings
- No Revenue Ruling, PLR or IRS FAQ resolves the classification of event contracts (as of July 16, 2026). — [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax); [TraderTax](https://www.tradertax.net/prediction-market-taxes); [Beancount, Sept 2026](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide)
- **OBBBA § 70114:** wagering losses are limited to 90% of losses, deductible only to the extent of gains and only when itemizing, effective for tax years beginning after Dec 31, 2025. The worked example: $50k winnings minus $50k losses leaves $5k taxable. Bills to restore the full deduction have been proposed but not enacted. — [Tax Foundation](https://taxfoundation.org/blog/gambling-losses-tax-big-beautiful-bill/); [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/kpmg-us/content/dam/kpmg/pdf/2025/gambling-losses-under-one-big-beautiful-bill.pdf); [Forbes, Jan 13 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nathangoldman/2026/01/13/gambling-tax-alert-new-law-cuts-loss-deductions-bettors-face-big-hit/); [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://fedgov.womblebonddickinson.com/post/102l1aj/client-alert-changes-to-gambling-loss-deductions-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bil)
- **Section 1256:** event contracts do not fit neatly into any category. The "regulated futures contract" test requires mark-to-market variation margin, but event contracts are usually fully collateralized up front. The § 1256(b)(2)(B) swap exclusion and notional principal contract analysis cut against it as well. Practitioners call 1256 treatment aggressive or minority. — [Seward & Kissel](https://www.sewkis.com/publications/twelve-fifty-kicks-prediction-markets-1256-contracts-and-other-tax-issues-to-consider/); [ABA Tax Times 2026](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/taxation/resources/tax-times/2026/prediction-market-contracts-involve-financial-instuments-taxation/)
- **§ 475(f) mark-to-market** requires trader tax status (a facts-and-circumstances test). It turns gains and losses ordinary, avoids the $3k capital-loss cap and the wash-sale rule, and must be elected by the original due date of the prior year's return. Whether event contracts are "securities or commodities" for § 475(f) is unresolved. — [TaxSlayer](https://support.taxslayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049969812-What-is-the-Mark-to-Market-Election); [CohnReznick](https://cohnreznick.com/insights/time-is-ticking-to-make-a-mark-to-market-section-475f-election)
- **1099s:** sources conflict. Kalshi's documented forms appear to be 1099-INT (interest), 1099-MISC (rewards and referrals) and limited crypto forms, with P&L delivered as an in-app statement. Other guides claim 1099-B or 1099-MISC for net payouts of $600 or more. KPMG reportedly expects 1099-B as typical. Polymarket issues nothing. I found no Novig-specific source in search. Income is taxable under § 61 regardless of whether a form is issued. — [Monaco CPA](https://www.monacocpa.cpa/prediction-market-tax); [CoinLedger](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax); [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/in-depth/116443/how-are-prediction-markets-taxed); [Camuso CPA (Novig taxes)](https://camusocpa.com/novig-taxes/)
- On Aug 15, 2026 the IRS warned bettors to use licensed platforms and said all winnings, from sportsbook or prediction market, are reportable income. — [Northeast Times](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/08/15/irs-warns-bettors-to-use-licensed-platforms-ahead-of-football-season/)
- **State:** NJ has a pending 9% surtax bill on prediction-market *operator* income, which is not law. A NY Division of Tax Appeals determination shows NY's itemized-deduction limitation (Tax Law § 615(f)) has applied to gambling losses (a 2003-year case). — [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/07/07/new-jersey-eyes-9-tax-on-prediction-markets/186329/); [NY DTA determination](https://www.dta.ny.gov/pdf/archive/determinations/821982.det.pdf)
- The Tax Policy Center notes that prediction markets may erode state wagering-tax revenue. — [TaxVox](https://taxpolicycenter.org/taxvox/could-prediction-markets-erode-state-gambling-tax-revenues)

### Inferences
- For a high-volume NY liquidity provider, gambling treatment is the worst case. Gross winnings would be included in AGI, with only 90% of losses deductible below the line. A near-break-even, high-turnover market maker could owe tax on phantom income equal to 10% of gross losses. Capital treatment (Form 8949, netting, $3k cap on net losses) is the most commonly taken position. A § 475(f) election, if available, best fits an active trader, but its eligibility is untested.
- "Professional gambler" (Schedule C) status no longer escapes the 90% cap, because OBBBA folds expenses into the wagering-loss limit. This comes from my background knowledge of § 165(d) as amended and needs CPA confirmation.
- NY State taxes residents on all income at up to 10.9%, and NYC residents add up to about 3.876%. That is background knowledge not verified in this pass. NY lets taxpayers itemize on the state return even when they take the federal standard deduction. That is also unverified here and important for gambling-loss treatment.
- Mitigation: choose one characterization consistently, document the rationale with a CPA, keep trade-level logs (exports from every venue), and consider a timely § 475(f) election (due about April 15, 2027 for tax year 2027) only after professional review. Make quarterly estimated payments, since no withholding occurs.

### Gaps

## 5. Counterparty, custody and account-limiting risk

### Takeaway
On CFTC DCMs and DCOs, customer funds are required to be segregated (Kalshi: Part 22 cleared-swaps customer segregation at Kalshi Klear). They are not FDIC-insured as platform balances, and in an insolvency recovery is governed by Bankruptcy Code commodity-broker liquidation and CFTC Part 190, with possible shortfalls. Insolvency of an event-contract venue is untested. On exchanges, winners are not "limited" the way sportsbooks limit them, but position limits, compliance holds and state geofences can restrict access.

### Inferences
- Historical futures-industry failures, such as MF Global in 2011 and Peregrine in 2012, show that segregated funds can be misused and recovery can take months. This is background knowledge, not sourced in this pass. Even full recovery would mean a liquidity freeze for an active trader.
- DCM non-discrimination principles (CEA "impartial access" core principle) mean exchanges should not limit winners the way sportsbooks do. Exchange-side risks are instead position limits, fee changes, and market-maker program terms that can favor designated LPs. This is inferred and not verified for Novig or ProphetX.
- **Mitigation checklist:**
  1. Spread capital across venues and keep balances at about the working margin needed.
  2. Withdraw profits to a bank regularly.
  3. Prefer venues with documented Part 22 / DCO segregation, and read each DCO's rulebook and customer acknowledgment.
  4. Monitor dockets (S.D.N.Y. Kalshi and Ludlow cases, NY v. Kalshi, NY v. Polymarket, 2d Cir., SCOTUS No. for NJ v. Kalshi, 4th Cir.) and keep the ability to flatten positions within a 30-day geofence window.
  5. Avoid long-dated sports futures when a NY ban is possible.
  6. Do not use VPNs or location spoofing. Nevada investigators' allegations show geofence circumvention is policed, and it would breach venue terms.
  7. Keep full trade logs for tax, and engage a CPA on characterization and a possible § 475(f) election.
  8. Track the final CFTC Rule 40.11 rule and any SCOTUS cert grant.
