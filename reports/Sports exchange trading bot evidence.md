# Sharp-line taking survives; most other bot strategies don't

For a New York trader with $100k, the public evidence best supports one strategy: **selective pregame taking of Novig and Kalshi prices that beat a de-vigged Pinnacle fair value after fees**. It should use a strict filter for when the reference line is stale or late news is pending, and bankroll-aware sizing. Cross-venue locked arbitrage is real but thin. Fees, depth, and differences in settlement rules eat most of the margin, so it works as an opportunistic add-on, not a profit engine. Pure market making on major lines is dominated by institutions such as Susquehanna and Jump, and **no verified record exists of a small independent sports market-making bot being durably profitable**. Hedged market making has no public performance evidence at all. Latency and steam chasing is a race against firms whose data is faster than yours; it should be dropped. Two things outweigh any strategy choice. The first is **New York legal risk**: the state has sued Kalshi (July 31, 2026) and Polymarket (Sept 24, 2026), and a federal judge has ruled against Kalshi. The second is an **unresolved Novig fee question**. If Novig charges pregame takers 0.03·P·(1−P), that cuts about 1.5 points off a 2.5% edge at even money and about 0.75¢ off every locked pair that has a Novig taker leg. A realistic target is low-five-figure annual profit with a meaningful chance of a losing year. It is not a bankroll-doubling machine.

## These findings rest on search snippets, not full pages

Read everything below with one limitation in mind. **The research environment's network proxy blocked direct page fetches for nearly every relevant site, including all of Reddit, the Kalshi and Novig documentation, arXiv full texts, and the Bet Angel forum.** Every finding comes from search-engine snippets and paper abstracts that cite those pages, and a snippet can paraphrase, truncate, or be out of date. Practitioner discussions on Reddit (r/algobetting, r/Kalshi, r/Novig, r/sportsbook) could not be read at all. That is the biggest hole, because those threads are where first-hand profit-and-loss and venue-behavior reports would live.

Confidence is highest for legal events, which are covered by multiple outlets and primary press releases. It is moderate for fee formulas: sources agree on the structure but conflict on current coefficients. It is lowest for depth, fill rates, latency, and realized returns, where the answer is usually "no public data." Many claims come from vendors selling arbitrage scanners or bots. This report labels those claims and never relies on one alone.

ProphetX is New York-based and launched "nationwide" on June 18, 2026, but no source confirms that nationwide includes New York residents ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/prophetx-launches-nationwide-302804384.html)). **Sporttrade is not available in New York** ([BettingUSA](https://www.bettingusa.com/sports/reviews/sporttrade/)), so it is out of scope for execution. One source conflicts on the date of the AG's Kalshi suit (some snippets say January 2026). The weight of evidence, including CNBC's dated article, points to July 31.

The legal picture is split, and it cuts against New York residents. In April 2026 the Third Circuit held that the CEA likely preempts New Jersey law ([Detroit News](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/business/2026/04/06/new-jersey-cant-regulate-kalshi-prediction-market-appeals-court-rules/89484816007/)). New York sits in the Second Circuit, where the only ruling so far went against Kalshi. **No enforcement action against individual traders was found.** The state's suits target operators. The practical risks are operational, and they are concrete.

**Sudden geofencing.** An injunction or a Second Circuit loss could force Kalshi or Novig to block New York users with little notice. Open pregame positions would then be stuck or force-closed, and a locked pair could lose one leg's venue before the game starts.

**Settlement and withdrawal friction.** Only settled cash is withdrawable on Kalshi, and ACH takes 1–4 business days ([OddsAssist](https://oddsassist.com/prediction-markets/kalshi-deposits-withdrawals-payout-speed/)). A shutdown during a busy weekend therefore ties up capital for days. The evidence does not show what happens to user funds if a venue is ordered to stop serving a state. No source documents a frozen-funds event, and that absence is not reassurance.

**Asymmetric venue loss.** The Kalshi and Polymarket suits are the most advanced. Novig's own lawsuit is an admission that it sees the Kalshi action as an imminent threat to itself. A realistic scenario is losing one venue while keeping the other, which would strand the cross-venue strategies first.

**Tax.** If income is treated as gambling, the 2026 OBBBA rules cap loss deductions at **90% of losses**. A trader who wins $20k and loses $20k would owe tax on $2k of phantom income, and Kalshi does not issue a comprehensive 1099-B ([Monaco CPA](https://www.monacocpa.cpa/post/prediction-market-taxes-kalshi-polymarket-robinhood); [Camuso CPA](https://camusocpa.com/kalshi-tax-reporting/)). For a high-turnover bot with thin margins, this can turn a small pre-tax profit into an after-tax loss. The treatment is debated (Section 1256, gambling, or ordinary income), and it needs a CPA opinion before scaling.

Kalshi outages add another operational risk. During an October 2025 college-football outage, the API kept working while the app did not. **Kalshi later refunded users whose resting orders were filled by API traders during the downtime** ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/18/kalshi-down-for-some-users-during-college-football-games.html)). In practice, a bot's best fills during a venue incident can be reversed after the fact, and that can unhedge a locked pair.

## Fees decide which strategies live, and the Novig fee is unresolved

The fee structures differ in kind, not just in size. Kalshi, Polymarket US and (possibly) Novig charge a coefficient times P·(1−P), which peaks at 50¢. ProphetX and Sporttrade instead take about 2% of net winnings.

| Venue | Taker cost | Maker cost | Confidence and conflicts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kalshi | ceil(0.07·C·P·(1−P)), **1.75¢ at 50¢**, 1.47¢ at 30¢, 1.12¢ at 20¢ | 0 by default; 0.0175·P·(1−P) on designated markets | Structure is solid ([Kalshi fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf); [Kalshi Help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823805-fees)). Whelan says makers on most sports markets pay a quarter of the taker fee ([Substack](https://finemarginskarlwhelan.substack.com/p/prediction-markets-vs-sportsbooks)). Sources also disagree on per-contract versus per-order rounding. |
| Novig | **0.03·P·(1−P) (0.75¢ at 50¢), OR taker fee only on live and parlays, OR no fees** | 0; maker credit on live fills | Direct conflict, see below |
| Polymarket US | θ·P·(1−P), sports θ = **0.05** or **0.06** | Rebate (−0.0125) | [Polymarket US fees](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees) vs [Laika Labs](https://laikalabs.ai/prediction-markets/kalshi-vs-polymarket-fees-comparison) |
| ProphetX | 2% of net winnings on straights (1.5% VIP tier) | Same | [ProphetX Help](https://prophethelp.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/26975338569489-Understanding-Fees) |
| Sporttrade | 2% of profits | Same | Not available in NY |

**The Novig conflict needs a direct answer from Novig before any threshold is finalized.** Three readings exist, and two of them cite the same Novig Help Center page ("Fees on Novig"). In one, takers pay 0.03·P·(1−P) on everything and makers pay nothing ([Novig Help](https://support.novig.com/en/articles/16195057-fees-on-novig); [OddsAssist](https://oddsassist.com/prediction-markets/novig-fees/)). In another, the same page is summarized as charging takers only on live in-game trades and parlays, so **pregame is free**. CBS Sports' 2026 NFL guide says Novig "doesn't charge any trading fees" ([CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/prediction/news/nfl-prediction-apps/)). A plausible explanation is that the fee arrived or changed with the June–August 2026 DCM relaunch. The trader's belief that pregame straights are free is consistent with two of the three readings, but it is not confirmed. The OddsAssist summary also says API fees equal app fees.

The fee would matter most to Novig taker orders. A 0.03·P·(1−P) fee cuts measured edge by roughly fee ÷ price:

| Novig price | Fee per contract | Edge lost | A 2.5% measured edge becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 75¢ | 0.56¢ | ~0.75 pp | ~1.75% |
| 50¢ | 0.75¢ | ~1.5 pp | **~1.0%** |
| 30¢ | 0.63¢ | ~2.1 pp | ~0.4% |
| 25¢ | 0.56¢ | ~2.25 pp | **~0.25%** |

These figures are this report's arithmetic from the cited formula. For longshots, the fee would nearly wipe out a 2.5% threshold. The same fee takes about 0.75¢ out of any locked pair that has a Novig taker leg near even money, so a pair the engine now scores as exactly 1¢ locked would net about 0.25¢. That is inside the range of rounding and mapping error. The Novig maker loop is unaffected under every reading, because all sources agree Novig makers pay nothing pregame.

**Kalshi: the engine already handles the fee correctly.** One research note warns that "a 2.5% raw threshold loses money on Kalshi as a taker": buying 2.5% below a 50% fair value at 48.8¢ costs 50.5¢ with the fee, for an expected value of about −1%. That warning **does not apply to this engine**, which computes Kalshi edge after the taker fee (effective price = P + 0.07·P·(1−P)). The remaining Kalshi fee issue is rounding. Fees round up to the cent, so a single contract at 50¢ pays 2¢ rather than 1.75¢. The $10/$100 canary orders will therefore be charged proportionally more than full-size orders. If the engine models the fee as a continuous per-contract value, canary P&L will look slightly worse than production would. The engine should model the rounding exactly.

## Taking against de-vigged Pinnacle is the best-supported edge

This is the strategy with real academic backing. Kaunitz, Zhong and Kreiner took mispriced outliers against a consensus fair price. Their backtest returned **3.5% over 56,435 bets**, and a small live test returned **8.5% over 265 bets** before bookmakers limited their accounts ([arXiv 1710.02824](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02824)). Pinnacle's own position is that consistently beating its closing line is the best marker of a winning bettor ([Pinnacle](https://www.pinnacle.com/betting-resources/en/betting-strategy/what-distinguishes-winning-from-losing-bettors/6bt2g5bmd43myskt)). Exchanges remove the limiting problem that ended Kaunitz's live run. Novig, ProphetX and Kalshi are peer-to-peer and advertise no limits on winners ([XCLSV](https://xclsvmedia.com/novig-review-2026-peer-to-peer-sportsbook-sharp-bettors/)). What they add instead is fees and professional counterparties.

There is one structural reason to expect a small edge on Kalshi specifically. Bürgi, Deng and Whelan find a strong favorite-longshot bias: contracts under 10¢ lose more than 60%, and **contracts above 50¢ earn small but statistically significant positive returns** ([GWU WP 2026-001](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf)). Bartlett and O'Hara find that Kalshi traders buy YES about 61% of the time in single-name markets, where YES wins about 32% ([Stanford Law](https://law.stanford.edu/2026/04/21/adverse-selection-in-prediction-markets-evidence-from-kalshi/)). So a Pinnacle-anchored engine should most often flag Kalshi favorites as cheap. That is consistent with the literature rather than a data error.

Three caveats temper this.

**The reference must be fresh.** Exchange market makers "are all marking from the same sportsbook" ([Veriphix](https://veriphix.substack.com/p/the-price-nobody-set)). Pinnacle (PS3838) is the stated reference for Polymarket sports as well ([StartPolymarket](https://startpolymarket.com/strategies/market-making/)). When the engine sees an exchange price 2.5% off Pinnacle, the most common explanations are that Pinnacle just moved and the engine's polled feed has not caught up, or that the exchange moved first on news. Both are adverse selection. REST-polled odds feeds typically carry at least a second of latency, while vendor streaming feeds claim sub-second delivery ([OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/how-often-odds-apis-update/); [Odds-API.io](https://odds-api.io/blog/odds-api-vs-opticodds)). The TheRundown plan's actual update cadence was not found in the notes. That makes a **"who moved first" filter** the most valuable addition: take only when the Pinnacle price has been stable for a set window and the exchange quote is the one that is off.

**Edges above about 6–8% are usually errors.** Practitioner guides consistently place real +EV against consensus in the **+1% to +6% range** and treat double-digit edges as stale or mis-mapped ([OddsIndex](https://oddsindex.com/guides/expected-value-calculator)). Users of commercial +EV tools report persistent false positives from bad line data ([Trustpilot OddsJam](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/oddsjam.com)).

**The choice of de-vig method matters at the extremes.** On low-margin Pinnacle two-way markets, the multiplicative, power and Shin methods differ by **0.1–0.5 points on average but up to 2.4 points at heavy favorites or longshots** ([SharkBetting](https://www.sharkbetting.com/blog/devig-explained)). Academic comparisons favor power or Shin over multiplicative normalization ([Clarke et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326510904_Adjusting_Bookmaker's_Odds_to_Allow_for_Overround); [Štrumbelj](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169207014000533)). At prices beyond about 75/25, the method alone can create or erase a 2.5% edge.

Two findings argue for humility about Pinnacle as ground truth. Practitioners say **Circa often leads US props and NFL lines**, and Unabated builds fair value from a speed-to-close-weighted blend rather than from Pinnacle alone ([Unabated](https://unabated.com/tools/core/unabated-line); [Pikkit](https://pikkit.com/blog/which-sportsbooks-are-sharp)). One practitioner also calls Novig and ProphetX prices "VERY sharp" when liquidity is deep, citing more than $25k resting on a prop ([Alex Monahan on X](https://x.com/AlexMonahan100/status/1974164718538363018)). When a deep Novig book disagrees with Pinnacle, Pinnacle is not automatically the one that is right.

Timing matters too. Informed flow concentrates around lineup news. NBA injury reports and NFL inactives land about 90 minutes before start, and NBA lineups, MLB pitcher scratches and NHL goalie confirmations cluster closer to game time. The engine's 1-minute taker cutoff means it keeps taking through the window with the most news-driven mispricing. Kalshi's removal of athlete-injury markets at the CFTC's request shows how central injury information is to this game ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/sports-betting/2026/kalshi-injury-betting-nfl-cftc-1234943772/)).

## Locked arbitrage is real, thin and full of hidden basis risk

Cross-venue price gaps exist. The best academic study finds **persistent 2–4% execution-aware deviations** across equivalent markets on ten venues. The authors attribute them to structural frictions and conclude that arbitrage "becomes capital-intensive or unenforceable" ([Gebele & Matthes, arXiv 2601.01706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01706)). That figure is an average across all categories, not a measure of fillable US pregame sports gaps. The only sports-specific microstructure study examined Polymarket NBA markets on a single venue. It found executable arbitrage **"exceedingly rare"**: seven in-game episodes with a **median life of 3.6 seconds** ([arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00864)). Gaps reported for liquid NBA games are **1–2¢** ([XCLSV](https://xclsvmedia.com/kalshi-vs-polymarket-arbitrage-2026-nba-finals-sharp-bettors/)). A Kalshi taker leg plus a Polymarket US taker leg near even money costs roughly 3.25¢ in fees, so taker-taker arbs between two P·(1−P)-fee venues rarely clear. The trader's Novig-plus-Kalshi pairing is better positioned, because only one leg pays a P·(1−P) fee if Novig pregame is free. Window-duration claims (12.3 seconds falling to 2.7 seconds; "73% of profits go to sub-100ms bots") come from vendors with no primary data ([Turbine](https://www.turbinefi.com/blog/prediction-market-arbitrage-latency-speed-2026)). Another vendor claims 15–30 seconds ([ClawArbs](https://clawarbs.com/prediction-markets/)). The honest summary is seconds to tens of seconds, and depth at the gap price is unknown.

Differences in settlement rules are the real danger in a "locked" pair.

**Ties.** Kalshi settles tied games at **50¢ per side** where ties are possible ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/how-to-bet-nfl-on-kalshi)).

**Postponements.** Kalshi uses a 48-hour window. Beyond it, cancelled or long-postponed games settle at a **"fair market price" rather than voiding at cost**. One GitHub issue documents a void settled at 0.29/0.43/0.28 ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-postponed-game-rules); [GitHub](https://github.com/alexander-bain/bainluck/issues/7035)).

**Ambiguous outcomes.** In the Super Bowl LX halftime market, Kalshi settled at the last traded price (26¢/74¢) while Polymarket resolved YES at $1 ([OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-vs-polymarket-settlement-rules)).

Novig's rules for ties and postponements were not found. If Novig voids postponed games at cost while Kalshi settles at fair value, a pair locked at 1¢ can lose tens of cents on a rainout. That matters most for MLB. The engine should refuse locked pairs in any market type whose tie and postponement rules it has not confirmed on both venues.

Capacity is the other limit. At the engine's caps, a fully filled 1¢ lock on about $1,000 per leg near 50¢ earns roughly $20. Capital sits split across venues until settlement, and it can only be rebalanced by slow ACH. One anecdote describes a $50k arbitrage position netting $865–$1,221, "not significantly more profitable than parking the money in a savings account" (origin unverified; [MEXC summary](https://www.mexc.com/news/97855)). No verified profit-and-loss record from anyone running cross-venue sports arbitrage was found.

## Market making pays institutions, and hedging doesn't fix adverse selection

Kalshi sports liquidity is anchored by Susquehanna (since April 2024), Jump, Kalshi's own affiliate, and desks being built at DRW, IMC and Wintermute ([Kalshi blog](https://kalshi.com/blog/article/kalshi-kit-liquidity-sig-market-makers); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/06/06/a-massive-hiring-wave-reveals-trading-firms-are-no-longer-viewing-polymarket-as-a-niche-betting-tool)). Novig's affiliated trading arm, Manhattan Athletic Group, trades on its exchange behind an information barrier ([Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/sports-betting/2026/prediction-market-maker-affiliate-odds-1234884140/)). That makes it directly relevant to the trader's Novig maker loop, because an affiliate with better models or data is a likely counterparty. The most telling data point is that **Kalshi's in-house trading arm said it was "not profitable" on sports** while supplying under 6% of making volume ([InGame](https://www.ingame.com/kalshi-in-house-trading-arm-not-profitable/)).

The academic results that makers beat takers look encouraging but do not transfer. Becker measures makers at **+1.12% per trade** across 72.1M Kalshi trades ([jbecker.dev](https://www.jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)). Bürgi, Deng and Whelan find that makers lose less than takers, about 10% against 32%, in a sample dominated by longshots. Bartlett and O'Hara show that maker profits come from retail YES and longshot bias, which subsidizes the cost of being picked off by informed traders. That behavioral subsidy is weakest in near-50/50 pregame sides and totals, which is exactly where the trader operates. It is also earned by makers in aggregate, most of whom are fast professionals.

The individual records are negative. The canonical documented Betfair market-making bot **lost money live despite a profitable backtest** and was shelved ([mildbyte part 7](https://mildbyte.xyz/blog/project-betfair-part-7/)). The most-cited open-source Polymarket market maker warns that it "can lose money" ([poly-maker](https://github.com/warproxxx/poly-maker)).

Where small market makers do earn, it is through **incentive programs, not spread capture**. Kalshi's open Liquidity Incentive Program pays for resting depth, capped at **$1,000 per market per day** and shared pro rata ([Kalshi Help](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program); [Navnoor Bawa](https://www.navnoorbawaresearch.com/p/kalshi-publishes-one-liquidity-subsidy)). Its sealed program, up to $50k per series per week, requires a Market Maker Agreement and a reverse auction.

Hedged market making means resting an order on Novig and hedging on Kalshi when it fills. It has **no public performance evidence, positive or negative**. Its main flaw follows from basic microstructure. A resting order fills when someone wants your price, and that is most likely just after the reference market has moved. By then the hedge venue has often already repriced, so the trader is adversely selected on both legs. Kalshi's order-rate tiers add a structural disadvantage: about 10 orders per second at Basic and 30 at Advanced, while higher tiers are volume-gated or assigned ([Kalshi docs](https://docs.kalshi.com/getting_started/rate_limits)). The trader starts behind the institutions on cancel speed at exactly the moment it matters. Throughput is fine for pregame; speed when news hits is not.

## Latency and steam chasing is a race this trader cannot win

Every documented latency success lives in crypto short-duration markets, not pregame sports. It was also short-lived. One wallet reportedly turned $313 into about $438k on Polymarket 15-minute crypto markets ([Bitget](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560605137629)). When Polymarket changed its taker delay and introduced dynamic fees in February 2026, "the entire class of pure latency arbitrage strategies stopped working the same day" ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/lkto1m/february-2026-changed-polymarket-forever-heres-what-happened-to-my-bots-numbers-2fi5)). Reports conflict on the exact delay timeline (500ms removed in February versus 250ms cut to 50ms in August 2026).

On sports, a hobbyist Kalshi NFL bot built on ESPN's win-probability API failed because professionals' feeds were **"at least 30 seconds faster"** ([HackingTheMarkets](https://hackingthemarkets.com/anatomy-of-a-kalshi-nfl-trading-bot/)). Steam chasing in pregame means reacting to Pinnacle moves before the exchange reprices. That puts the trader in a race against Susquehanna-type market makers who read Pinnacle directly and are paid to keep quotes current. Leaving the pregame-only rule and the in-play stop in place is correct.

## Realistic profits are low five figures with real downside

No verifiable source reports annual returns for a $100k pregame US-sports bot. The population data are sobering. Of about 2.5M Polymarket wallets, **84.1% lost money** and **only 0.015% made at least $5k in each of four consecutive months** ([The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/research-and-opinion/polymarket-profitability-report-april-2026)). On Betfair, fewer than 0.5% of customers were ever profitable enough to pay the Premium Charge ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betting_exchange)). Betfair also shows the long-run venue risk. Once an exchange matures, it taxes consistent winners: first a 20–60% Premium Charge, and from 2025 an Expert Fee of 20–40% ([Racing Post](https://www.racingpost.com/news/britain/betfair-exchange-to-introduce-new-commission-system-for-2025-as-premium-charge-is-dropped-a7wbg0v4GCAJ/)). US venues are already changing fees and programs every few months.

The scenario below is illustrative arithmetic, not evidence. Assume the +EV taker fills about 5 positions a day, averaging $600 each (consistent with quarter Kelly on a 2.5–4% edge and the $1,000 cap), over 300 days. That is about **$900k of turnover**. Realized edge is usually well below the threshold edge, because of stale references and selection effects. At 1% realized, the expected profit is about $9k. At 2%, it is about $18k. The standard deviation of annual profit near even-money prices would be around **$23k**. That implies roughly a 1-in-3 chance of a losing year at 1% edge and 1-in-5 at 2%, before about $4.8k a year for TheRundown data (per the requester's figure) and any gambling-tax drag. Locked arbitrage adds perhaps a few thousand dollars if depth cooperates. Market making should be budgeted at zero until paper data say otherwise. If Novig fills are routinely much smaller than the cap, turnover and profit fall in proportion. Depth at best price on Novig and Kalshi single-game markets is **not published anywhere**. That makes it the most important number to measure during paper trading.

Several things should be avoided. **Do not** add in-play trading, steam chasing, or any strategy whose edge depends on reacting faster than Pinnacle-watching market makers. **Do not** add Polymarket US or rely on ProphetX from New York until access and legal status are clear. Polymarket was sued yesterday, and ProphetX's New York availability is unconfirmed. **Do not** scale hedged market making on the strength of the maker-return academic papers, since they describe aggregate professional makers and longshot-biased flow. **Do not** treat vendor window and profit statistics as benchmarks.

Several questions remain open.
1. What is Novig's actual pregame taker fee?
2. What are Novig's rules for ties, postponements and voids, its API rate limits, and its order types?
3. How much depth sits at best price on Novig and Kalshi single-game markets pregame?
4. How many seconds does TheRundown's Pinnacle feed lag Pinnacle itself?
5. What realized closing-line value do fills above the 2.5% threshold actually show?
6. Does the Second Circuit panel grant or deny Kalshi's injunction pending appeal?
7. What happens to New York users' open positions and balances if a venue is ordered to stop serving the state?
8. What do first-hand Reddit and forum reports say about bot P&L and account treatment on Novig and Kalshi? They could not be read in this research.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the trader's question. The engine already includes most of the defensible strategies, and its main constraint is what surrounds the strategies: which fee Novig actually charges, how stale the reference line is, which settlement rules differ between venues, and whether New York leaves the venues standing. The strongest edge on these exchanges comes from their plumbing: no limits on winners, a favorite-longshot bias that makes Kalshi favorites cheap, and retail resting orders that lag a sharp line. Model sophistication and speed are not where the edge is. Every structural edge in this market has been taxed away or competed away once it became visible, so the edges above are temporary.

That makes paper trading the key next step, not a formality. The purpose of the next few weeks should be to measure closing-line value, markouts and fillable depth on the trader's own fills, since no public source has published those numbers. A realized closing-line value of a point or two, at meaningful depth and with legal exits in place, justifies scaling beyond the canary. Anything less says the edge belongs to someone faster.
